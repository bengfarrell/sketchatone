## Start with a single chord

Sketchatone starts with a configurable chord, so you can strum without setting up any chord changes first.

When you want to change chords, you have a few options.

<h2 id="a-chord-at-the-touch-of-a-button">Use tablet buttons or computer keys</h2>

Most drawing tablets have buttons on the side or on the stylus. Those buttons can change chords, leaving your strumming hand free to keep moving.

A computer keyboard works too. Plug in a full keyboard or a small USB numeric keypad and assign chords to its keys. You can use tablet and keyboard buttons together.

The [button and action guide](/about/action-rules/) explains how to assign a chord change or another action to a press.

## Set up a progression

Map individual chords to buttons, or use a group of buttons to move through a progression. You can choose your own chords or use diatonic mode, which gives you chords from the same key.

You can start with just two chords and add more later. The reference covers [chords and progressions](/about/chords-and-progressions/) and the available [chord modes](/about/chord-modes/).

<h2 id="two-hands-one-instrument">Use a MIDI keyboard</h2>

If you'd rather choose notes on piano keys, connect a MIDI keyboard. Hold a chord with one hand and strum those notes with the other. Change what you're holding, and the strummed chord follows.

You can also send the keyboard to a separate sound in your DAW, pairing a sustained pad with a plucked strum. For the setup details, see [playing with MIDI input](/about/scales-and-midi-driven-mode/).
